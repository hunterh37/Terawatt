Action: what this build does and how it works

Rack to the Future is one shared model drawn at seven scales. You set a scenario. The engine turns it into numbers. Seven level views, the control panels, the business panel and a Vision Pro mode all redraw from those same numbers. You can zoom from one chip out to the Earth fleet and on to orbit without leaving the page.

The story the demo tells. Case B is 50M agent developers. On 2035 GPU racks it needs about 1,790 racks, roughly 1.4 GW of IT power. The Earth grid gives 500 MW across all sites. The twin shows that gap, which check fails first, and what closes it. These are engine outputs for the start preset at build time. The app itself always reads live metrics.

One model, seven scales

The scales run from the chip up to off-world. Module 1 is the Business module: Earth fleet, site and off-world. Module 2 is the Engineering module: chip, rack, hall and building. Picking an object zooms one step finer. Pressing zoom out, or tapping the parent in the breadcrumb, moves one step coarser. Off-world sites skip the building and the hall and go straight from site to rack.

How it is wired

The engine is a pure function, meaning its output depends only on its input. It takes a scenario and returns one metrics object. It has no page code and no 3D code. The catalog and the sources list sit beside it, and every input number carries a source id. Every level module follows the same small interface, so panels, lenses and picking need no per-level wiring: it builds a 3D group, restyles it when the numbers or the lens change, lists what can be picked, and returns a legend, a readout and one caption sentence. A new level only needs an entry in the level list (LEVELS in src/contract.js, plus its loader in LEVEL_LOADERS in src/main.js) and in the zoom-out chain (parentOf in src/main.js and the matching breadcrumb path).

What the engine computes

Each line below is a formula in words, with its source. Slide numbers win over every other source. The Terawatt app engine (the Terawatt visionOS app) fills gaps where the slides are silent.

  • Demand. Peak tokens per second equal users times peak tokens per second per user, split into interactive and background work (d.demand). The peak hour runs 2x for chat, 2.5x for agents and 1x for background work. A token is a small piece of text the model reads or writes.
  • Rack capacity. Chips per rack times tokens per second per chip (d.capacity). One 2035 GPU decodes about 58,000 tok/s of chat or 11,700 tok/s of agent work. Prefill (reading the prompt) and decode (writing the answer) are two pools of racks.
  • Racks needed. The smallest rack count whose capacity covers peak demand (d.capacity).
  • Facility power. IT power (the power the computing equipment draws) times PUE (d.facility). PUE, power usage effectiveness, is facility power divided by IT power. It equals 1 plus the cooling overhead plus 0.04 (s15.pue), and it moves between a cold-climate and a desert-climate column with the site's temperature (a.climate).
  • Water. Facility energy times 1.8 L per kWh, for evaporative cooling only (d.water).
  • Five-year cost. Racks plus buildings plus energy over 43,800 hours plus carbon plus launches (d.cost5yr). Carbon is priced at $100 per tonne. Buildings use $17.6M per MW; the Terawatt app engine uses $11M per MW, and a slider covers both.
  • Launch and radiators. Launch cost equals IT MW times 18 t per MW times dollars per kilogram times a multiplier of 1 for low Earth orbit, 6 for the Moon and 15 for Mars (d.launch). Radiator area is IT MW times 1,080 m2 (d.radiator).
  • Joules per output token. Facility watts divided by decode tokens per second served (d.jtoken). The challenge frames the goal as joules per task (s22.framing), and this is the per-token view of the same idea.
  • Money. NPV (net present value: future cash flows discounted to today), IRR (internal rate of return: the discount rate at which NPV is zero) and payback (years until cash flow turns positive) come from year-0 capex (up-front spending on racks, buildings and launches) and five years of revenue minus opex (running cost) (d.finance). The discount rate is 10% a year, an assumption.
  • Break-even price. The flat monthly price per user that makes five-year NPV zero: (opex plus capex divided by an annuity factor, which turns five equal yearly payments into one sum today), divided by users served, divided by twelve (a.breakeven). It needs no guess about 2035 prices.
  • Chip heat flux. Compute watts divided by compute die area (d.heatflux). Compute watts are the chip's power minus the power of its HBM (high-bandwidth memory) stacks, and the die sizes come from s28.dies. A die is one slice of silicon cut from a wafer.

Lenses

A lens recolours every object at the current scale. The camera does not move. Five lenses exist, and each one is defined at every scale, or says so when it does not apply.

  • Heat. On the fleet, each site's PUE. On a chip, heat flux per square centimetre (colouring the dies and the HBM, the high-bandwidth memory stacked beside them) on a display scale that runs to 1,500 W/cm2. That scale is only a display range, not a limit.
  • Power. Facility MW per site. At rack scale, rack kW.
  • Cost. Energy price per MWh per site, and rack cost at rack scale.
  • Sentiment. The stance of one recent local news article per site (ext.sentiment). It is a sample, not a poll. At rack and chip scale it reads "n/a at this scale".
  • Water. Water use per site, and cooling litres per kWh at rack scale (d.water).

Six checks, in a fixed order

Six constraint checks run on every change: Grid, Budget, Serving, Cooling fit, Latency and Water. A banner shows the first failing check in red, with its message. Tapping it moves the view to the level named in the check's cause, so a failing check always leads to where it can be understood. The Water check uses a simple rule: it fails when evaporative cooling runs at a desert-climate site (a.water_rule). That rule is an assumption, and the sources list says why.

The year slider

The slider swaps in each year's rack catalog, from 2025 to 2035, and keeps the current rack counts. Only a preset or the Size to demand button re-sizes the fleet to the demand. In 2025 the GPU rack is the GB200 NVL72, with 132 kW and 72 GPUs. In 2035 it is 792 kW and 144 chips. Between the anchors, rack power and chip count are interpolated geometrically (steady percentage steps) so that a hotter rack is never less efficient per watt than an older one (a.year). LPU racks (chips with very fast but small memory, see s14.memory) and air-card racks enter the catalog in 2030; before that the view says they are not yet available. A line chart in the business panel does re-size the same demand for each year, and shows racks needed and energy per output token (d.projection).

Presets as a guided tour

A preset is a saved scenario. The Tour button plays a nine-step story of about a minute, built from live numbers. It opens on the Earth fleet with the start preset (fit-grid), then zooms to a site, a hall, a rack and a chip. It then applies Decode on LPUs (lpu-decode) and LPUs with overflow to orbit (lpu-orbit), returns to the fleet to read the break-even price, and ends on the same demand on 2025 racks (today-2025). The whole argument can be shown without touching a slider. The tour does not use All of Case B on Earth (challenge-b) or the three other presets, which stay in the scenario panel, and it returns to the start preset when it ends.

Read the table top to bottom. Fitting Case B into the grid serves a third of demand. Moving decode to LPUs helps. Orbit nearly closes the serving gap (97%) but breaks the budget. Putting everything on Earth breaks the grid. Using 2025 racks for the same demand breaks it far worse.

Apple Vision Pro

The same scene runs in WebXR, the browser standard for headset and passthrough experiences. In Safari on Vision Pro, the Enter VR button starts a session. The table appears in front of the viewer at desk height. A pinch on an object zooms in, using the same pick code as the desktop pointer. A floating panel behind the table switches lens, year and preset, and has zoom out and a life-size toggle that scales the hall so racks read near real size. By design, every XR call sits behind a feature check. Where WebXR is missing, the page shows a note to open it on Vision Pro, and everything else still works.

Source discipline

Every number a viewer can see comes from the engine metrics, the catalog or the sources list, and every number in the panels names its source with an "i" badge. Tapping the badge names the source: a slide, the technical spec, the Terawatt app engine, a published page with a link, a formula, or an assumption with its reason. Where a published page was used, it was checked against the raw page. The app makes no requests to other origins and loads no outside library.

Known AI campuses appear as markers on the Earth fleet. The Cleanview US totals count data centers with reported capacity: 1,372 operating and 2,689 planned, shown as "US operating (with MW)" and "US planned (with MW)". They are credited with a link to Cleanview US Data Center Map. The individual Cleanview markers stay in a local checkout and are not drawn in the public build because of the data licence.

The copy rules are checked too: no shipped text file outside vendor/ contains an em-dash, and every readout row names a source id that exists (AC13).

Acceptance criteria

These fourteen criteria are the contract. QA tests them, and this page quotes them as written.

AC1  Zoom in
  Given the Earth fleet level
  When I pick the West Texas site
  Then the view shows the West Texas site and the breadcrumb reads "Earth fleet / West Texas"

AC2  Zoom out
  Given any level other than off-world
  When I press zoom out or tap the parent in the breadcrumb
  Then the view moves one level coarser

AC3  Off-world path
  Given the off-world level
  When I pick low Earth orbit
  Then the site level shows the orbital site, and picking it goes straight to a rack (no building or hall)

AC4  Lenses
  Given any level
  When I switch the lens to Power
  Then objects recolour, the level card legend shows the power scale, and the camera does not move

AC5  Year slider
  Given the start preset
  When I move the year to 2025
  Then the GPU rack becomes the GB200 NVL72 (132 kW, 72 GPUs) and energy per output token rises

AC6  First failing check
  Given the start preset (Case B in 500 MW)
  Then the constraint banner shows Serving as the first failing check
  When I tap it
  Then the view goes to the level named in its cause

AC7  Grid binds
  Given the preset "All of Case B on Earth"
  Then the Grid check fails because Earth facility power is above the 500 MW cap

AC8  Every number has a source
  Given any number in the level card, business panel or scenario panel
  When I tap its "i" badge
  Then a popover names its source: a slide, the spec, the Terawatt app engine, a published page with a link, a formula, or an assumption with its reason

AC9  Business case
  Given the Earth fleet level
  Then the business panel shows NPV, IRR, payback, the break-even price per user per month beside reference plan prices, and a money-flow chart
  When I change the subscription price
  Then NPV and IRR update

AC10 Projection
  Given the business panel
  Then a 2025 to 2035 chart shows racks and energy per output token for the same demand

AC11 Existing data centers
  Given the Earth fleet level
  Then known AI campuses (for example Stargate Abilene, 1.2 GW planned) appear as markers with sources
  And the Cleanview US totals (1,372 operating, 2,689 planned) are credited with a link

AC12 Vision Pro
  Given Safari on Apple Vision Pro
  When I tap Enter VR
  Then the table appears in front of me at desk height, a pinch on an object zooms in, and an in-world panel switches lens, year and preset

AC13 Copy hygiene
  Given the repository
  Then no file contains an em-dash, and every readout row names a source id that exists

AC14 Chip workbench
  Given the chip level
  When I add compute dies or HBM stacks
  Then chip power and heat flux update and the heat lens recolours the dies
