Rack to the Future in one zoom

One looping animation. It pulls back from a single chip to the Earth fleet, fills the grid gauge until it overflows, lifts compute to orbit, and ends on the break-even price. Numbers shown are engine outputs at build time for the preset named in each caption (the start preset unless stated), or come from the sources list. Each one carries its source id in the caption. The app itself always reads live metrics.

Storyboard

Nine beats. The first six pull the camera back one scale at a time. Geometry is schematic, not to scale.

  1. Chip. One package with 4 GPU dies and 4 HBM stacks[s28.dies]. The heat lens warms the dies.
  2. Rack. A GPU rack rises from the table: 792 kW and 144 chips[s12.racks]. Coolant flows beside it.
  3. Hall. 64 racks[a.hall_racks] in rows with hot and cold aisles. Orange racks prefill, blue racks decode.
  4. Building. Halls in a grid under a cutaway roof, with a substation and a cooling plant. Direct liquid cooling gives a PUE of 1.07 in a cold climate and 1.14 in a desert[s15.pue].
  5. Site. A ghost building sized to its megawatts. West Texas has a 45 ms round trip[s13.rtt].
  6. Earth fleet. Six Earth sites share one grid cap of 500 MW[s9.setting].
  7. Grid gauge. Case B (50M developers[s11.cases]) needs 1,790 racks[d.capacity]. On Earth that draws 1,554 MW[d.facility] against the 500 MW cap[s9.setting]. The bar overflows and racks lift off.
  8. Orbit. In the "LPUs on Earth, overflow to orbit" preset, 499 MW of compute sits in low Earth orbit[d.placement], 40 ms round trip[s13.rtt]. The Moon is 2.6 s and Mars 25 min[s13.rtt].
  9. Break-even price. $34.45 per user per month[a.breakeven], beside $20 and from $100 plans[ext.sub_price]. Start preset, engine output at build time.
